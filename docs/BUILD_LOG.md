## 2026-09-17 · M8.1 · Feedback round 1 (four lenses, ACTIVE on the fork)

Lenses: program boundary and integer math; web transaction routes (malformed, malicious, auth and abuse); services state, concurrency and failure injection; supply chain and program authority. Every finding below was reproduced by the lens or by this pass before it was fixed; each is tagged as the lens tagged it.

**CRITICAL, CONFIRMED, fixed.** F1: the assignment product `P` rounded down at every exercise and a slot's `assigned` was derived from `denom − p`, so writers could be assigned more in total than was exercised; the last `settle_writer` then reverted on the settlement vault and `close_series` was blocked forever (reachable for about 200 lots of self-trade churn parking `P` at its floor). Fix: `P` rounds up, a slot's unassigned rounds up and assigned is its complement, two rescales since a snapshot are divided exactly (`P_SCALE²` fits u128), and each settle leg pays `min(owed, vault)`. The rounding dust now lands on the collateral leg of the last settler instead of bricking the series. Regression: `tests/qa_boundary.rs` (both writers settle, vault pays exactly the strike on the assigned units, series closes) and four invariant tests in `math.rs`; the random walk's invariant flipped to the new direction. Also fixed with it: F2 u128 overflow in `split` for writers above 340k lots (complement form), F3 decimals capped at 19.

**HIGH, CONFIRMED, fixed.** `/api/tx/send` relayed any signed transaction through the app's RPC: now only transactions whose top-level programs are the Roster program (plus Jupiter, token and system programs for a Protected Buy) are relayed, `lastValidBlockHeight` must be near the chain's, and the confirm loop has a deadline. `/api/positions` failed for every wallet past 100 series (`getMultipleAccountsInfo` cap): chunked. The geo gate read two headers with a client-settable one first: one header, `GEO_HEADER`, empty means absent. Every expiry blacked out Tier 2 markets for the grace period (expired series hold their slots until close): caps sized to `(expiries + 1) × 3 × 2` and Tier 2 pre-creates the next expiry within grace of the nearest. The program keypair `programs/roster_finance/roster_finance-keypair.json` was tracked in git and pushed: untracked and ignored; **mainnet must use a fresh program id** (`docs/SEEDING.md` step 1); the history still holds it until rewritten, which is the operator's call. Keeper fee farmable by self-cranking dust positions across wallets: only the registered keeper (the pause authority) is paid, anyone may still crank (test in `p1b.rs`). Silent volatility floor: a breaker off the fork unless `VOL_FLOOR_OK=1`; vol cached hourly.

**MEDIUM, CONFIRMED, fixed.** Quoter cancelled before verifying it could repost (collateral idle, drained wallets never reposted): funding is settled first and a slot that cannot top up quotes what it holds. Breakers left stale asks resident: every breaker and a missing price now pull the treasury's asks. Pre-IPO mark refresh failure dropped every price for a tick: the cache keeps the last marks. `create_series` cap griefing (free, permissionless, rent back only after grace): Tier 1 and 2 creation limited to the authority and a `series_creator` (the quoter; `Protocol` gained the field inside its reserved bytes, zero means authority only), Tier 3 stays open. Shell scripts executed `.env` and let it override the caller's environment; the deploy script only guarded URLs containing "mainnet": both fixed, anything but the local fork needs the approval flag. Open image proxy: issuer logo hosts only. Price age measured against the chain clock (time-travelled on the fork): wall clock. Malformed build requests returned raw JS errors and negative amounts wrapped silently: a request parser and a `u64` helper. Swap quotes served any mint with any slippage: listed markets only, slippage clamped. `init_protocol` is first-caller-wins: deploy and init are one step in the runbook (the `ProgramData` constraint is the mainnet-grade fix, left open). Indexer recorded events from reverted transactions and marked unserved signatures as read: fixed, and block times fall back to the chain clock.

**Throughput, found by the browser flow.** With twenty-two markets the services walked them one at a time and the keeper sent one crank at a time, so a Friday where a whole grid expires took minutes to settle and a person's receipt arrived long after their transaction. Three bounded fan-outs, all sharing `mapLimit` in `@roster/core`: the tick prices and cranks four markets at once, the keeper settles four series and four writers at once, and the indexer reads eight transactions at once and still processes them in slot order. Measured on the fork: settles went from about two a minute to about seventy, and a full pass from minutes to about 100 seconds. Because cranks must never crowd out a person's transaction (the first fan-out made a Protected Buy time out on the RPC), the keeper also holds a single ceiling of six builds-and-sends in flight across every market, `KEEPER_SENDS_IN_FLIGHT`.

**Receipts.** `/v1/positions/:wallet` now pulls events from the chain before it answers, and a caller arriving mid-pull waits for that scan instead of skipping it, so a receipt is there on the first refresh after signing rather than on the indexer's next scheduled pull. A pull slower than two seconds is logged with its count, which is the lag signal P8 will alert on.

**A fee mint could never give its rent back (found by the same throughput work, fixed).** With the keeper finally reaching every expired series, `close_series` failed on every First Print market with Token-2022 error `0x23`: "an account can only be closed if its withheld fee balance is zero". A transfer-fee mint keeps part of every transfer *in the receiving account*, so a series vault on tKalshi, tOpenAI, OPENAI or SPACEX always carries a withheld balance and the program's `close_account` was refused. `close_series` now harvests those withheld fees to the mint first (permissionless, and they belong to the issuer, not to us), which needed the underlying mint account to be mutable. Without it a fee-mint series was immortal: rent never returned and the market's `max_live_series` filled with dead series until no new strike could be listed. Covered by a new fork test (`tests/e2e/p4-first-print.test.ts`): after expiry the writer settles, the vault's withheld amount is asserted non-zero, the close succeeds, the rent returns and the mint's withheld total rises by exactly that amount. Verified on the fork after the redeploy: the series that had been refusing to close, including the one diagnosed by simulation, all closed and reclaimed rent.

## 2026-09-26 · M10 · Slow and failing page loads: one read path made robust

**Measured first.** From outside, `/api/roster` took 1 to 8 s for 214 KB and every app page waited on it; `/api/cluster` took 0.8 s for 149 bytes because it pulled the whole roster; the landing took 6.5 s on a cold hit; `/api/vaults` spiked to 12 s; and roughly one call in ten came back as the 500-byte reconnecting answer because the chain behind it had run past the 8 s timeout. The chain was: web health probe → web roster read → two event reads, and on the services side each `/v1/roster` did two live RPC reads (the clock sysvar and the protocol account) on a paced endpoint. Sparklines were 152 KB of the 214 KB: 230 unrounded points per market.

**Services.** The tick keeps the chain clock offset and the protocol account; `/v1/roster` never awaits the RPC, its serialized answer is reused for five seconds and dropped at every tick; sparklines are 96 points rounded to six significant digits; answers over a kilobyte go out gzipped; the vaults answer is kept warm by the tick and served stale while one refresh runs behind it, so no request waits on the RPC.

**Web.** A stale-while-revalidate memo per process over every services read (roster 4 s fresh, 60 s stale; health, vaults and events likewise); `rosterData` no longer probes health first; `/api/cluster` reads health only; cache headers on roster, cluster, vaults and pre-IPO (harmless on Railway, where there is no edge cache, useful behind one); `fetchJson` in the browser retries with backoff and a timeout, treats a reconnecting roster as not settled, and the hooks keep the last good data and refresh quotes every 20 s while the tab is visible; the landing is rendered at most every 15 s instead of per visitor.
